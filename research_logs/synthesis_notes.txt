# Research Log: When Does an Ensemble Surrogate Get It Wrong?

## 1. General ML Findings
- **Ensemble Spread vs. Error**: Recent work ("Ranked Right, Sized Wrong") explicitly states that the spread of random-initialization ensembles is NOT guaranteed to reflect the magnitude of predictive error, even in training regimes. This supports the "breakdown" part of the hypothesis.
- **Adversarial Exploitation**: There is a massive body of literature on "adversarial examples" where optimizers (attackers) find points where the model is confident (low uncertainty) but wrong. While this is mostly in CV/NLP, the principle of "optimizer exploitation of blind spots" is a core tenet of adversarial ML.
- **Epistemic Requirements**: "On the Calibration of Epistemic Uncertainty" notes that epistemic uncertainty must decrease as N increases. The user's interest in how the "gap" evolves with N aligns with this formal requirement.

## 2. Structural/Mechanical Engineering Findings
- **Surrogate-Assisted Optimization (SAO)**: Many papers use NSGA-II with Kriging or ANN (e.g., "Surrogate-Assisted Multi-Objective Optimization of Stilling..."). Most report "high agreement" or "reliable predictions," but they typically report average errors across the domain or at a few verified points.
- **Lack of "Failure Analysis"**: There is a noticeable gap in literature documenting *where* the optimizer fails relative to the uncertainty signal in mechanical design. Most papers focus on efficiency gains, not the "hidden failures" of the uncertainty metric.
- **Blind Spots**: One paper on knee contact mechanics mentions "critical blind spots" where models focus on anatomical variations but overlook neuromuscular patterns—this is a domain-specific blind spot, not necessarily an optimizer-driven one.

## 3. Uncertainty Calibration
- **Calibration Gap**: "Uncertainty Quantification of Surrogate Models..." (arXiv 2408.09881v3) notes that data-driven surrogates typically lack uncertainty quantification, limiting reliability in safety-critical apps. This implies that when they *do* use it, it may not be well-calibrated.
- **Ensemble Calibration**: Research exists on decomposing aleatoric vs epistemic uncertainty in engineering, but the specific "optimizer exploitation" angle is not explicitly termed as such in these journals.
